# Evaluate the certificate the calling service actually receives

The issuer controller runs in the relevant cluster. Put the internal signing authority on the protected secrets service. Public names use a separate public-issuer function. The offering service declares whether its transport uses TLS. Each caller has its own expected DNS names and trust anchors. Certificate requirements follow those facts, independently of the product providing each function.

The example fixes June 10 as its evaluation day. A leaf is valid from its start up to, but excluding, its end. The warning window includes an end exactly seven days away. Walk the issuer chain to a root trusted by this caller. Another caller trusting the same root supplies no authority. A complete read of the relevant service establishes a missing certificate. An unread service remains unknown. A plaintext service has no certificate requirement.

Renewing a leaf changes its fingerprint. A verified replacement matching the expected name, trust chain and current validity carries no drift finding. The disposable loop recorder captures independent ordinary client handshakes before and after reconciliation and records the presented fingerprints, trust results and evidence references. No private key or credential is recorded.
